Before they arrive (5 minutes)

  • Start the app (python app.py) and open http://127.0.0.1:8050. Check that it loads.
  • Choose the organisation: Best: their own. On the login page use Start your organisation with their organisation's public website, a few minutes before they arrive, so the profile is filled in. Upload one or two of their public reports to Source documents if they have them. Otherwise: the demo closest to their field (literacy → Riverbend Reads, health → Harbor Light, food → Prairie Roots, veterans/housing → Cedar Ridge, STEM/youth → Bright Orbit, arts → Tidewater).
  • Run one Discover search yourself first, so results are cached and the session doesn't wait on the AI.
  • Have a screen recorder or a phone ready for 2–3 screenshots (only if they agree).
  • Close other tabs and notifications. Don't explain the app in advance.
0:00 – 2:00

1. Welcome and consent

Thanks for doing this. I'm testing a tool I built, not you, so there are no wrong answers. Please think out loud as you go: what you're looking for, what you expect, what surprises you. I may stay quiet so I can see where the app is unclear. It takes about 15 minutes.
Is it OK if I note your comments and quote you anonymously in a class report, by role only (for example "a development director at a mid-sized nonprofit")? And may I take a screenshot or two of the screen, not of you?
2:00 – 4:00

2. Background: how they work today

These answers are the "before" numbers. Ask for their honest typical time, not a best case.

4:00 – 7:00

3. Task 1: Find a grant and decide whether to pursue it

Imagine it's Monday morning and you want one new grant worth pursuing. Using this app, find one and decide whether it's a Go. Tell me when you've decided.
Success: they open Discover, run or read a search, open a result, read the fit (dial and bars, "Why it matched", "Your history with this funder"), and either Add to pipeline or reject it with a reason. Don't help unless they're stuck for 60 seconds.
Do they understand the fit %? Do they open the dimension bars? Do they trust the recommendation? Do they notice the funder icon or "Applied before"?
0:00
7:00 – 10:00

4. Task 2: Draft an answer and improve it

Now open an application that's being written and get a first draft of one question. Then make it better using whatever the app suggests, until you'd be comfortable sending it to a colleague.
Success: Applications → open one → pick a question → ✦ Draft with AI → read "Where each fact comes from" → Review suggestions → Accept or Reject at least one → (optional) Evidence tab. Stop the clock when they say it's good enough to share.
Do they check the sources? Do they spot [MISSING: …] gaps? Do they trust or edit the draft? Do they understand Accept/Reject and Undo?
0:00
10:00 – 13:00

5. Task 3: Prepare an update for your board

Your board meets this week and wants to know where fundraising stands. Use the app to find out what needs attention, and produce something you could hand to them.
Success: either asks the chat assistant (bottom right), e.g. "What should I work on this week?", and/or goes to Reports → Board update → (optional) ✦ Write summary with AI → ⬇ PowerPoint deck or ⬇ Word document, and opens the file. Stop when they have the file open.
Do they find Reports? Do they read "Waiting on people" on Home? Would they actually send the deck? What would they change in it?
0:00
13:00 – 15:00

6. Wrap-up questions

Thank you, that was really helpful. Is there anything else you'd want it to do?

7. Quote capture

Write their exact words the moment they say something striking, positive or negative. The report needs one strong quote; a critical one shows honesty.
